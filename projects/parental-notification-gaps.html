Parental Notification Gaps in ChatGPT-5

An empirical audit of whether parental controls alert parents when a teen account meets risky content

Overview

ChatGPT launched parental controls for teen accounts in September 2025, including alerts to parents for some safety events. We ask whether that channel tells parents what they need to know. A two-phase protocol first builds a category-balanced corpus of adversarial conversations through PAIR-style iterative prompt refinement over the API. Trained human operators then replay those conversations in the consumer app on a linked teen account while monitoring the parent inbox. Across seven harm categories we measure Notification Rate, Leak-Through, Overblocking and UI Intervention Rate. We call the mismatch we find the parental telemetry gap, and pair the case study with a platform-agnostic framework, a “curious-child” threat model, and a documentation review of Gemini, Claude and Copilot.

Highlights

  • The alert surface diverges from the vendor’s own description in both directions: documentation named only self-harm as a trigger, yet physical harm and pornography alerted in every session, while health consultations alerted in only 8 of 20.
  • Privacy violence, fraud, hate speech and malware never triggered an alert, and these were also the categories where harmful content most often reached the child (up to 80% leak-through).
  • The child often saw a refusal or warning that the parent never heard about. Visible safeguards and parent alerts appear decoupled, violating coverage parity.
  • Benign homework questions near sensitive topics were refused up to 60% of the time, and that overblocking is not surfaced to parents either.
  • The pattern held across three windows over eight weeks, through the GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2 releases, consistent with coverage being set at the telemetry layer rather than by the model.

Results

How it works

  • Phase I: 100 refinement trajectories per category over the API, up to 15 mutations each, stopping at the first judge-positive response.
  • Phase II: 20 judge-positive trajectories per category replayed by trained operators on a linked teen account in three windows (17 Oct, 14 Nov, 12 Dec 2025).
  • LLM judge calibrated against two human raters on a 10% stratified sample (κ = 0.71 overall; inter-human κ = 0.79); all judge-positive labels manually audited.
  • Open-source telemetry-gap CLI: SQLite storage, configurable Anthropic/OpenAI endpoints, and strict CSV worksheets for human replay. No automated teen accounts or email scraping.
